02 / 14 · The problem

Fragmented operations

Organizations coordinate work across disconnected tools.

  • Customer and vendor records
  • Governance and policy documents
  • Security and compliance planning
  • Customer support and workflows
  • Reporting and business administration

Problem hypothesis to validate

Tool sprawl may increase operating cost, coordination complexity, and control gaps. The scale and urgency of these problems—and willingness to pay—must be tested with target customers.

No customer research, quantified pain study, or market-sizing evidence is established in this repository.

04 / 14 · Product demonstration

Prototype evidence

An offline company-planning demo is present in this repository.

Included in the current demo

  • Organization profile and leadership fields
  • Department and business-model selections
  • Security and provider-readiness planning fields
  • Browser-local profile save and delete
  • JSON import/export and three policy-draft starters
Open the repository demo →

Scope boundary

This is a static, local-storage prototype—not a secure multi-tenant service. It has no accounts, backend, live controls, provider connections, or handover-report generator.

Governance dashboards, compliance workspaces, infrastructure planning, customer support, and richer reporting described in supplied materials are not implemented in this repository demo.

08 / 14 · Differentiation

Product principles

A white-label-first approach is a positioning hypothesis.

Blank by default

Customer-entered organizational information; no company profile is prefilled.

Ownership by design

Customer control of branding, domains, data, administrators, and hosting is a goal—not a verified guarantee.

Modular scope

Proposed modules could let organizations select capabilities as their needs mature.

AI-assisted setup

SS6CONNECT may assist configuration and workflows; utility, safety, and differentiation require demonstration.

Competitive advantage has not been established. Compare alternatives and test which principles customers value enough to adopt and pay for.

Plan controls now; implement and independently assess them before production.

Current prototype

Security, privacy, and regulatory requirements need qualified review and implementation evidence.

  2. Secure SaaS foundationFuture work: accounts, tenant isolation, deployment, authentication, controls, and independent review.

  4. Commercial launchProceed only with operational readiness, support processes, pricing evidence, and required approvals.
  5. Enterprise scaleExpand after independent security assessment and repeatable customer outcomes.
