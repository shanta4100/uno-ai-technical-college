Financial data boundaries

Six domains, connected without conflating evidence

The proposed architecture separates authoritative records from operational and analytical data. Each domain needs its own source, purpose, controls, and evidence.

1 · System of record

Posted accounting records and financial statements, governed by accounting policy and controls.

2 · Treasury & money operations

Cash, settlement, and payment workflows; fund movement remains with authorized institutions/providers.

3 · Market intelligence

Time-stamped observations for equities, bonds, FX, commodities, and digital assets; not accounting facts.

4 · Research & forecasting

Models and scenarios with recorded inputs, assumptions, versions, horizons, and uncertainty.

5 · Validation history

Proposed records of forecast outcomes, actual comparisons, errors, corrections, and human overrides.

6 · Governance

Identity, policy, security, risk, approvals, audit, and compliance review across the other domains.

Accounting facts ≠ market observations ≠ forecasts ≠ research scenarios ≠ AI recommendations. Analytics and simulations must not silently become posted accounting records.

Consolidated volume blueprint

Operational events connect to accounting through controls

These flows describe proposed requirements. Actual accounting treatment and tolerances depend on applicable policy, standards, jurisdiction, and entity facts.

VolumeProposed flow and control principle
3 · Transaction processingCapture → verify → validate → classify → assess tax/risk → approve → post → execute through authorized channels → reconcile → audit. A proposed transaction record can carry entity, branch, department, project, counterparty, currency, jurisdiction, risk/approval/reconciliation state, supporting-document reference, and audit reference. Unbalanced or unauthorized entries should be rejected, logged as exceptions, and retained for review. Segregation of duties separates vendor creation, payment approval, reconciliation, and audit access.
4 · General ledgerJournal header and lines → validation (debits = credits) → posting → trial balance and statements. Reconcile control accounts to their subledgers.
5 · Procurement & APRequest → budget check → approval → vendor → purchase order → receipt → invoice match → payment workflow → ledger → audit. Proposed controls: approved vendor and valid invoice checks; duplicate/mismatch holds; independent bank-change verification; no silent budget override; AP subledger reconciliation. Match tolerances depend on policy and risk. Before payment, review available cash against payroll, taxes, debt, reserves, and treasury policy. Risk scores and anomaly flags prompt review, not accusations.
6 · AR & revenue assuranceProposed flow: customer/credit → contract/order → delivery → billing event → invoice → receivable → collection → cash application → bank reconciliation. An invoice or forecast alone does not establish revenue recognition. Proposed controls distinguish valid customer/credit, documented billing event, cash application plus reconciliation, gross AR less allowance equals net AR, and AR-to-GL reconciliation. Expected billing should be sourced from contracts, usage, tariffs, milestones, orders, or approved pricing—not forecasts. Credit-model inputs/version/reviewer should be retained; AI must not autonomously approve credit or freeze customers.
7 · Inventory & manufacturing (recommended next volume)Proposed scope: FIFO/weighted average, batch/lot/serial tracking, aging, EOQ/reorder/safety-stock planning, BOM/WIP and standard/actual costing, variance analysis, lead times, freight/landed cost, and obsolescence. Inventory subledger-to-GL reconciliation remains a required design control.
9 · Payroll & workforceWorker → approved compensation/time → calculation → review/approval → payment provider → subledger → ledger → reconciliation. Payroll calculation is distinct from payment authorization; payroll expense subledger should reconcile to its GL control account.
10 · Treasury (future)Cash positioning, liquidity, debt, FX, investment tracking, covenant monitoring, and provider integrations are future scope. Metrics may include cash conversion cycle, liquidity, free cash flow, burn/runway, and debt-service coverage. The platform may prepare/reconcile workflows; authorized banks/providers move funds.
Financial mathematics are analytical tools, not accounting evidence. Measures such as working capital, CCC (DIO + DSO − DPO), NPV, IRR, WACC, VaR, or forecast error require defined inputs, assumptions, methods, and review.

Proposed procurement and payment controls

Vendor → contract → purchase order → receipt → invoice → approval → payment → settlement → reconciliation. Vendor risk dimensions may inform review, but are not evidence of misconduct. Match exceptions require defined tolerances and documented disposition.

Budget and treasury review

A proposed availability calculation is approved budget less commitments and actual spend. Insufficient budget routes to a hold, approved transfer, or documented exception—not a silent override. Payment proposals may be reviewed against payroll, taxes, debt service, reserves, and liquidity policy.

Segregation and exception handling

Design intent separates requester, preparer, approver, payment releaser, reconciler, and auditor where practical. Each material action should record who, what, when, why, evidence, approval, and outcome. Each exception should retain an owner, due date, cause, corrective action, and resolution history.

Illustrative accounting workflow

A reviewed invoice may propose debit expense, inventory, or asset; debit recoverable tax where applicable; and credit AP. Settlement may propose debit AP and credit cash. Actual accounts and recognition depend on approved accounting policy and transaction facts.

Proposed governance mechanics

Resolve rules. Preserve lineage.

Accounting and tax treatments require context and current authoritative sources; a general framework cannot determine the correct treatment for a particular entity or transaction.

Accounting policy context

A proposed policy registry would identify entity, jurisdiction, reporting period, reporting basis, effective date, source authority, and policy version for review. Rules require validation before use; this page is not accounting advice.

Tax rule context

Tax workflows should account for jurisdiction, entity, tax type, effective date, current authority, amendments, and guidance. Simulations and calculations are not tax filings or authority determinations.

Evidence lineage

Proposed traceability: report → statement → account → journal → subledger → business event → source document → authorization. Forecast history could retain model version, horizon, timestamp, observed outcome, and error measures such as MAE and directional accuracy. A hash or signature alone does not establish completeness, correctness, or trusted provenance.

Continuous accounting concept

Event → validate → classify → propose journal → reconcile → route exceptions. Detection does not authorize a change; corrections should preserve prior records and their review history.

AML/CFT and audit support

Research concepts include monitoring, evidence, investigation support, and authorized review. Tests may examine duplicate transactions, journal patterns, approvals, and segregation of duties. An exception is not proof of fraud, and AI analysis is not an audit opinion or an enforcement decision.

Cryptography and digital assets

Cryptography may support authentication and integrity without implying cryptocurrency use. Digital-asset protocol, accounting, tax, and legal treatment are separate questions.

Authentication design note

Any future implementation should use a purpose-built password-hashing function (for example, Argon2id or bcrypt), not raw passwords or plain SHA-256. Authorization should validate identity, role, permission, entity scope, and endpoint; this proposal is not evidence those controls exist.
