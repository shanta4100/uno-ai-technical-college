## 1. Executive conclusion

The United States may be a strategically relevant location to evaluate semiconductor and advanced-manufacturing projects because of its research institutions, industrial customers, public programs, workforce infrastructure, and policy interest in supply-chain resilience. These are diligence topics—not proof that a particular project is commercially viable, eligible for public support, or able to reduce supply-chain risk.

A credible project assessment should test whether a defined product and service scope can attract customers and operate competitively, while accounting for capital and operating costs, construction and permitting schedules, supplier access, workforce, utility needs, customer qualification, and applicable law.

ATMP/OSAT and advanced packaging are potential areas for study. They should not be assumed to be lower risk, lower cost, or faster to commercialize without process-specific engineering and customer evidence. Front-end wafer fabrication is a separate investment decision with materially different infrastructure and capital requirements.

## 4. Staged investment framework

The time ranges below are planning horizons, not project commitments.

### Stage 0 — U.S. opportunity screening

**Focus**

- Verify the sponsoring entity, proposed ownership, decision rights, and available capital.
- Define product scope and target customers before selecting a site.
- Conduct customer, competitor, market, and qualification research.
- Identify candidate states and sites using documented criteria.
- Map federal, state, local, environmental, safety, labor, tax, export-control, and permitting requirements.
- Build an initial risk register and source/assumption register.

**Gate:** Do not proceed to detailed engineering without a credible customer hypothesis, responsible sponsor, and a plausible regulatory and infrastructure path.

### Stage 1 — Foundation and feasibility

**Potential scope:** workforce planning, ATMP/OSAT process definition, quality-system design, supplier assessment, site diligence, preliminary cleanroom and utility concept, and digital-manufacturing requirements.

**Required evidence:** product-specific engineering, vendor quotations, customer qualification pathway, schedule, workforce model, site and utility studies, environmental and safety scoping, and scenario-based financial analysis.

**Gate:** Independent technical, market, legal, environmental, and financial review must support a bounded investment decision.

### Stage 2 — Bounded pilot or initial facility

Proceed only with written authorization, committed financing, site control, required permits, qualified personnel, customer-approved requirements, equipment acceptance criteria, safety controls, and quality processes. A demonstration or pilot is not commercial production.

### Stage 3 — Advanced packaging expansion

Assess advanced packaging or additional product classes only after prior capability, customer demand, process control, yield, reliability, technical talent, and return assumptions are demonstrated. New technology requires a separate business case and qualification plan.

### Stage 4 — Long-term ecosystem development

Research, supplier localization, broader automation, and additional semiconductor capabilities may be considered through independent future decisions. Expansion is not automatic and should not be justified solely by sunk investment.

## 5. Incentive and capital framework

### 5.1 Potential financing components

A project may evaluate sponsor equity, commercial debt, customer arrangements, equipment finance, and public support where legally available. These are potential financing categories, not confirmed commitments:

> Private capital + financing + customer or supplier arrangements + eligible public support = possible capital structure

Model each source’s timing, conditions, repayment, restrictions, clawbacks, and risk allocation. Public support does not establish customer demand, operating competitiveness, profitability, or project completion.

### 5.2 U.S. programs require current eligibility review

The supplied material refers to the Advanced Manufacturing Investment Credit (AMIC/Section 48D), CHIPS grants and loans, state incentives, and advanced-packaging programs. No specific credit percentage, award, loan, grant, state incentive, or program eligibility is asserted here.

Before including any support in a project model, obtain current authoritative guidance and qualified U.S. tax and legal advice on the taxpayer, eligible property, activity, location, timing, documentation, stacking restrictions, recapture or clawback, and interaction with other assistance. Keep unconfirmed support outside the base-case funding plan and test it only as a clearly labeled sensitivity.

### 5.3 Financial model requirements

Prepare a traceable model with:

- CapEx by land/site, building, process infrastructure, equipment, installation, commissioning, qualification, contingency, and construction financing.
- OpEx by staffing, utilities, materials, maintenance, technical support, quality, compliance, insurance, logistics, and training.
- Working capital, customer qualification duration, receivables, inventory, supplier terms, and ramp losses.
- Revenue by service and customer, distinguishing pipeline, letters of interest, qualification activity, forecasts, and binding contracts.
- Separate private return measures from public benefits such as skills, resilience, or supplier development.
- Scenario and sensitivity analysis for schedule, cost, demand, yield, utilization, pricing, workforce, power, water, materials, FX, financing, and incentives.

Report NPV, IRR, break-even utilization, cash runway, debt-service capacity, and downside funding needs only with disclosed assumptions and independently reviewable calculations. No dollar ask or project return is established by this framework.

## 6. Competitive pillars

### Pillar 1 — Incentive diligence

Create an eligibility matrix for federal, state, and local programs. Identify the responsible agency, controlling authority, application window, eligible costs, documentation, award status, restrictions, timing, and recapture provisions. Do not state that incentives “maximize” eligibility or reduce project CapEx until an authorized determination and amount are documented.

### Pillar 2 — Advanced packaging capability

Assess customer-specific needs for package architecture, chiplets, interconnects, thermal performance, test, reliability, materials, equipment, and qualification. Market growth or a claimed capacity bottleneck must be supported by a dated, relevant source and should not substitute for project-level demand evidence.

### Pillar 3 — AI and automation in manufacturing

Candidate uses include predictive maintenance, process monitoring, machine-vision inspection, scheduling, defect analysis, and digital-twin studies. Measure against an operational baseline. Any proposed production use must define authorized data, model version, performance and failure thresholds, human review, escalation, cybersecurity, and safe-state behavior.

No AI-generated result should make an unreviewed safety-critical process change. Potential outcomes such as higher yield, lower downtime, reduced cost, or faster learning are hypotheses to validate—not guaranteed benefits.

## 7. Utility, environmental, and site strategy

### Water and process infrastructure

Water requirements depend on the selected process. Some semiconductor processes require ultrapure water and substantial treatment; an ATMP/OSAT facility’s actual water profile must be established by process and site engineering rather than assumed from wafer-fabrication practice.

Evaluate source availability, quality, treatment, reuse, wastewater, discharge, monitoring, drought exposure, permits, and community impacts. Establish a water balance and environmental baseline before design commitments.

### Energy and reliability

Assess grid capacity and quality, connection schedule, redundancy, backup generation, power conditioning, electricity price and tariff exposure, emissions, and renewable options. Do not claim an energy advantage until site-specific evidence supports it.

### Permitting and construction

Map federal, state, local, environmental, building, fire, water, air, waste, utility, worker-safety, and other reviews applicable to the selected facility. Include realistic dependencies and contingency in schedule models. Coordination may improve visibility, but this framework does not propose bypassing independent review or statutory requirements.

## 10. Risk register

Project-specific risk ratings remain unassigned until a candidate site, scope, schedule, and data are available.

| Risk | Indicators to assess | Candidate mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Construction cost premium | Vendor quotes, local labor, equipment, contingency, cost escalation | Independent cost review, scope control, downside case |
| Permitting and schedule | Approval dependencies, public review, interconnect milestones | Early authority mapping, critical-path review, schedule contingency |
| Upstream supplier availability | Lead times, single sources, materials, maintenance, spare parts | Supplier mapping, alternatives, continuity and inventory analysis |
| Customer qualification and demand | Product fit, orders, concentration, qualification duration | Customer discovery, staged investment, demand stress tests |
| Workforce constraints | Skill availability, instructor capacity, retention | Employer-informed curricula and assessed training outcomes |
| Utility and environmental constraints | Power, water, wastewater, emissions, site hazards | Site-specific engineering and environmental review |
| Technology change and yield | Process maturity, equipment performance, quality | Product qualification, controlled ramps, independent technical review |
| Incentive uncertainty | Eligibility, award timing, clawbacks, changing rules | Exclude unconfirmed support from base case; legal and tax review |
| Cybersecurity, IP, and export controls | Data access, vendor exposure, controlled technology | Threat assessment, contractual controls, export and compliance review |

Assign an owner, probability and impact rationale, trigger, treatment, residual exposure, and acceptance authority once sufficient evidence exists. Never describe a risk as eliminated.
