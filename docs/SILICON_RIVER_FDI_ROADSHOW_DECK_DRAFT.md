# Silicon River — FDI Roadshow Deck Draft

**Status:** Narrative outline for discussion; not an investment offer or investor-ready financial model  
**Project status:** Bangladesh ATMP/OSAT concept under preliminary assessment  
**Funding ask:** Not set; requires independently reviewed scope, quotes, demand evidence, and financing model  
**Regional strategy:** Bangladesh–India collaboration is a proposed opportunity, not an established corridor or partnership

## Slide 1 — Opportunity

**Bangladesh as a specialized semiconductor packaging and testing hub supporting regional and global supply chains**

Evaluate a staged path beginning with workforce, market, site, and infrastructure diligence before any facility investment. No site, facility, investor, customer, or government endorsement is established by this deck.

## Slide 2 — Market context

Semiconductor demand and advanced packaging are potential investment drivers. Before presentation, insert current, cited data on:

- Global and regional semiconductor markets by relevant product and service segment.
- Packaging, test, HBM, chiplet, and advanced-integration demand and capacity.
- Customer qualification timelines, competitor landscape, and pricing evidence.

Use publisher, release date, page/table, geography, units, and forecast assumptions on the slide. Do not state a capacity shortage or a market forecast without direct sources.

## Slide 3 — Proposed Bangladesh capability path

Potential sequence for assessment:

1. Workforce and design-enablement programs.
2. Site, utilities, environmental, regulatory, and customer feasibility.
3. A bounded ATMP/OSAT investment, if a bankable case is established.
4. Advanced packaging or broader ecosystem options only after qualification and performance evidence.

This sequence is conditional. No facility construction, production start, or service capability is represented as active.

## Slide 4 — Potential demand sources

Potential sources to investigate—not guaranteed demand—may include regional electronics manufacturing, industrial automation, power and sensor applications, and global semiconductor customers seeking qualified packaging or testing services.

Evidence required: customer interviews, product specifications, letters of interest (clearly distinguished from orders), qualification requirements, expected volumes, pricing, competitive alternatives, and customer concentration. Do not imply captive demand, named customers, job protection, or productivity/yield improvements without evidence.

## Slide 5 — Location and ecosystem diligence

Present only verified findings on:

- Candidate sites and land tenure.
- Electricity quality, availability, redundancy, and connection timing.
- Water supply, treatment, wastewater, emissions, and waste handling.
- Transport, customs, communications, climate and hazard exposure.
- Supplier, equipment-service, materials, and labor availability.

No specific Bangladesh Hi-Tech Park or other site is assumed selected or approved.

## Slide 6 — Regional collaboration opportunity

Explore whether Bangladesh’s workforce, design, embedded-systems, and software capabilities could complement relevant Indian semiconductor manufacturing, packaging, research, and supplier capabilities.

Any India–Bangladesh corridor, MoU, university exchange, facility access, named-company relationship, incentive eligibility, or cross-border flow must be verified and authorized before inclusion as fact. Use “strategic regional collaboration opportunity,” not “secured partner,” “live corridor,” or “sovereign node.”

## Slide 7 — Technical concept

Potential ATMP/OSAT service mix to study:

- Assembly and packaging.
- Electrical test and reliability screening.
- Marking, inspection, and traceability.
- Advanced package integration only if customer needs and process qualifications support it.

The final scope depends on product mix, equipment, workforce, customer audits, IP, materials, utilities, export rules, and engineering studies. Do not use an unqualified units-per-day capacity claim.

## Slide 8 — Workforce and training

Map training requirements by role: design/test, packaging/process, quality/reliability, technician/operator, equipment/facilities, EHS, cybersecurity, and logistics. Establish headcount targets only from an approved process flow, shifts, automation assumptions, curriculum capacity, employer demand, and budget.

Track competency-assessed completions and employment outcomes; do not equate enrollment with trained or placed personnel.

## Slide 9 — Investment model and use of funds

No dollar ask or allocation is established. Present a use-of-funds table only after independent engineering and supplier quotations support it. Potential categories include site and facility, cleanroom/process utilities, equipment, installation and commissioning, training, working capital, safety/environmental controls, and contingency.

Show CapEx and OpEx separately. Identify which amounts are quotations, estimates, contingencies, and confirmed commitments. Do not include unverified tax holidays, VGF, duty relief, grants, or subsidies in the base case.

## Slide 10 — Commercial case and scenarios

Present customer-backed revenue assumptions and a transparent model of pricing, qualification, yield, utilization ramp, service mix, working capital, maintenance, energy, water, materials, labor, logistics, taxes, and financing.

At minimum, show:

- Reference case with stated assumptions.
- Delay and cost-overrun case.
- Lower-demand / utilization-downcycle case.
- Supply, FX, or policy-shock case.

Report break-even utilization, cash runway, NPV/IRR ranges, debt-service capacity, and sensitivities. Keep strategic resilience benefits separate from private returns.

## Slide 11 — Risk register

| Risk | Evidence needed | Candidate mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Permitting delay | Authority map and critical path | Early scoping and schedule contingency |
| Utility reliability | Site-specific power/water studies | Engineering redundancy and written utility diligence |
| Talent constraints | Workforce baseline and role demand | Staged training with competency assessment |
| Supply-chain dependence | Critical input and supplier map | Alternatives, inventory policy, regional sourcing where feasible |
| Technology and yield | Process and qualification evidence | Controlled commissioning, vendor acceptance, customer qualification |
| Demand cycles | Customer-backed demand and pricing | Multiple customers, flexible phasing, stress-tested working capital |
| Financing and incentives | Written terms and eligibility | Base case excludes unconfirmed support; model downside funding needs |
| Environmental, safety, and climate | Site baseline and applicable assessments | Avoid, minimize, mitigate, monitor, and maintain emergency plans |

Probability and impact ratings should be assigned only after documented assessment, with owner, date, rationale, and residual risk.

## Slide 12 — Milestones and measurable outcomes

Use milestone gates rather than promised calendar outcomes:

| Gate | Evidence of completion |
|---|---|
| Feasibility | Independently reviewed market, engineering, site, workforce, regulatory, environmental, and financial studies |
| Investment decision | Customer evidence, approved scope, financing, permits pathway, and documented go/no-go decision |
| Commissioning | Equipment acceptance, safety approvals, staff competency, process validation, and operational readiness |
| Commercial qualification | Customer acceptance, quality evidence, authorized operating status, and contracted demand |
| Expansion | Sustained operational and financial performance, additional demand, and a separately approved business case |

Targets such as “200 trained by Year 2,” “export operations by Year 5,” or “multiple customers” require an approved baseline, schedule, budget, owner, and evidence. Until then, they are not commitments.

## Slide 13 — Evidence-gated execution sequence

The proposed program is organized around three pillars: pilot validation, industrial capability development, and investment readiness. This is a planning sequence—not evidence that any pillar is active or approved.

1. **Clinical or industrial pilot validation:** Proceed only with a willing partner, approved scope, funding, safety and regulatory review, and predefined measures. A healthcare pilot can generate workflow, usability, data-governance, and human-review evidence for that clinical use case. It does not establish semiconductor product demand, manufacturing capability, yield, or facility economics.
2. **BIDA and semiconductor feasibility:** In parallel or after initial scoping, develop site-selection criteria, workforce and utility requirements, capital and operating estimates, an independently reviewed risk register, and a customer-backed business case. Confirm current incentive eligibility with responsible authorities; do not assume an incentive.
3. **Investment attraction:** Circulate an FDI deck only when claims are supported by relevant pilot results, completed feasibility studies, workforce plans, a transparent financial model, risk analysis, and verified funding or incentive pathways. If the deck concerns only the semiconductor project, healthcare pilot results should be clearly identified as separate and must not be used as evidence for semiconductor demand or performance.

### Refined external narrative

> This draft organizes the proposed program around three implementation pillars: pilot validation, industrial capability development, and investment readiness. The next steps are to define and, if approved, measure a bounded pilot; complete phased ATMP/OSAT feasibility; and pursue targeted investment only when the evidence supports it.

Do not use language such as “the code is written, and the river is flowing” to imply deployment. Distinguish planning, signed commitments, active work, and verified outcomes.

## Slide 14 — Investment proposition and next step

The investment rationale to test is whether staged ATMP/OSAT capability can achieve commercial viability while contributing to workforce development and supply-chain resilience. Resilience is a public or strategic benefit to measure separately; it does not substitute for returns, customers, yield, or cash flow.

**Requested next step:** agree whether to commission an independent feasibility study and confirm the evidence required before a funding request. Do not request or imply a specific investment amount until scope, quotations, customer evidence, and financing have been reviewed.

## Investor-disclosure checklist

- Verify corporate identity, authority, ownership, and financial disclosures.
- Source all market, policy, cost, employment, demand, and capacity figures.
- Disclose assumptions, uncertainty, downside cases, and conflicts of interest.
- Obtain permission for all partner, customer, government, university, and logo references.
- Separate proposed concepts, active work, signed commitments, and verified results.
- Obtain qualified legal, tax, engineering, environmental, and securities review before circulation.
