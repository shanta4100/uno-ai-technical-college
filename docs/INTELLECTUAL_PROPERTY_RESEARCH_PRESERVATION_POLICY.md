## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy proposes practices to preserve research and development history, support attribution and lawful collaboration, and govern responsible publication. It may apply to research papers, books, software, documentation, diagrams, datasets, videos, podcasts, educational materials, design specifications, prototypes, and knowledge systems maintained by the organization.

The archive should distinguish among:

- Ideas and proposals.
- Research and working materials.
- Prototypes and implementations.
- Test results and independently verified findings.
- Published works and archived releases.

These labels describe evidence and maturity; they do not determine legal rights.

## 2. Guiding principles

Integrity · Transparency · Evidence · Attribution · Preservation · Innovation · Responsible sharing

Preserve failures and revisions as well as successes. Do not falsify, backdate, or silently overwrite historical records. Clearly separate sourced facts, analysis, speculation, prototypes, verified results, and future aspirations.

## 3. Rights, ownership, and contributor attribution

Do not presume that the organization owns every work created under a program or stored in an archive. Before publication, licensing, transfer, or reuse, identify the creators and contributors and review applicable employment, contractor, collaboration, funding, assignment, and third-party license terms.

For each work, record as applicable:

- Author(s), creator(s), contributor(s), editor(s), and reviewer(s).
- Employer/client or commissioning party, if relevant.
- Applicable license, assignment, permissions, or other written agreement.
- Third-party content, open-source components, datasets, and attribution requirements.
- Known restrictions, permitted uses, and unresolved rights questions.

Founder or organizational attribution is historical context supplied by the parties. It does not by itself establish patent rights, trademark rights, copyright registration, legal ownership, inventorship, or priority of invention.

## 4. Research preservation and archive records

Preserve original drafts, working papers, design notes, calculations, diagrams, software revisions, relevant meeting records, and publication versions when appropriate and lawful. Apply data minimization and access controls: do not place credentials, private keys, unnecessary personal information, restricted source data, or confidential third-party material in a general archive.

Each major work should have an archive record containing:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Archive identifier | Stable internal reference |
| Title and work type | Name and category |
| Creator/contributor record | Attribution and role, subject to agreement |
| Created/released dates | Dates supported by available records |
| Version and status | Draft, research, prototype, implementation, testing, verified, published, or archived |
| Source and evidence references | Links, citations, test reports, and provenance |
| Rights and restrictions | Licenses, permissions, confidentiality, and retention constraints |
| Revision history | Changes, reasons, and responsible contributors |
| Integrity record | Hash algorithm, reported/recomputed value, time, and verification method |

A cryptographic hash can help detect byte-level changes to a particular file. A hash alone does not prove authorship, creation date, ownership, originality, or legal priority.

## 5. Source control and version preservation

Each release should identify its version, publication date, author/contributors, change summary, status, and applicable rights notices. Keep released artifacts distinct from working branches and preserve reviewable change history where appropriate.

Suggested status values:

`RESEARCH` · `PROTOTYPE` · `IMPLEMENTATION` · `TESTING` · `VERIFIED` · `PUBLISHED` · `ARCHIVED`

Use “verified” only with a documented verification scope, method, result, and evidence. A release label is not a certification or approval.

## 6. Publication and licensing

Potential publication formats include research books, historical works, educational books, reference guides, journal articles, technical papers, case studies, review papers, newsletters, podcasts, videos, and public lectures.

Before release:

1. Confirm contributor attribution and publication permissions.
2. Check third-party copyright, trademarks, open-source licenses, dataset terms, and image/audio/video rights.
3. Separate original results from cited material, interpretation, and speculation.
4. Complete technical and editorial review; add historical review when archival claims are made.
5. Obtain qualified legal review where rights, privacy, regulated subject matter, or public claims warrant it.
6. Record the authorized publication decision, version, date, and license.

Public educational or research release may be considered when rights and restrictions allow. Attribution does not replace permission where permission is required. No open license should be implied unless the release explicitly names one.

## 7. External contributions and collaboration

Agree in writing on roles, attribution, confidentiality, background IP, newly created work, permitted reuse, publication review, data handling, and dispute/escalation paths before substantial collaboration begins. Identify authors, contributors, editors, reviewers, and institutional affiliations accurately. Do not imply endorsement by a collaborator or institution without authorization.

## 8. Research ethics and publication accuracy

The archive should preserve material history and disclose meaningful revisions. Publications should identify whether statements are historical records, sourced facts, research concepts, prototypes, verified results, or future aspirations.

Do not overstate evidence, fabricate citations, remove material limitations, or present a model output as an established fact. Protect participant privacy and comply with applicable research ethics, consent, data-protection, records, and confidentiality obligations.

## 10. Preservation, access, and continuity

Potential storage locations include access-controlled local archives, cloud storage, source-control repositories, and offline backups. Select locations based on confidentiality, data residency, access control, durability, recovery objectives, and contractual requirements.

Define retention schedules, authorized custodians, backup/restore testing, encryption and key management, access logging, and secure deletion. Avoid keeping duplicate sensitive data without a defined purpose. Periodically verify archived files and recovery procedures; preservation is not guaranteed merely by storing a copy.

## 11. Governance and review

Before publication or transfer, route works through the applicable technical, editorial, historical, rights, privacy, and legal reviews. Record the decision-maker, date, conditions, unresolved issues, and released artifact. Review this policy periodically and when organizational structure, applicable law, funding, or collaboration terms change.

### Master publication archive record

For each released work, create or update a master archive record containing:

`Archive ID · title · publication date · version · author/contributors · classification · rights/license · source/evidence references · integrity record · change log · publication history`

Suggested classifications include Historical Record, Research Paper, Journal Article, Book Chapter, Educational Publication, Technical Blueprint, Concept Paper, Prototype Documentation, and Future Civilization Study. Choose the classification that accurately describes the work and its evidence; do not use “verified” merely as a publication category.
