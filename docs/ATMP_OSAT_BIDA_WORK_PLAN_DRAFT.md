# Preliminary ATMP/OSAT Feasibility and Work Plan

**Status:** Planning draft; not a BIDA application, approved project, or authorization to construct or operate  
**Proposed geography:** Bangladesh; site not selected  
**Schedule, cost, capacity, workforce, and customer status:** To be established through feasibility work  
**Applicant identity:** GNAIAAAC LLC as supplied; legal identity and authority to apply require verification

## 1. Purpose and decision sought

This work plan defines studies and stage gates for assessing a possible semiconductor assembly, testing, marking, and packaging (ATMP/OSAT) project. The immediate decision sought is whether to commission a properly scoped feasibility study and obtain guidance from relevant authorities—not approval of a facility, national-priority designation, incentive, site, capacity, or financing.

An ATMP/OSAT project is distinct from a front-end wafer fabrication plant. No design or engineering basis is yet available to specify a product mix, facility class, cleanroom classification, equipment bill, throughput, or investment amount.

## 2. Scope to investigate

Potential workstreams, subject to customer and technical evidence:

- Product and service definition: package families, assembly flows, test coverage, marking, inspection, traceability, and reliability requirements.
- Process and equipment engineering: process flow, capacity model, equipment specification, installation, commissioning, qualification, maintenance, and spares.
- Facility and utilities: site, cleanroom/environmental controls as required by selected processes, electrical quality and redundancy, water, wastewater, gases, chemicals, HVAC, fire safety, waste handling, and emergency response.
- Customer and market validation: target customers, qualification requirements, demand evidence, pricing, contract structure, and competitor alternatives.
- Workforce: researchers, process and packaging engineers, test and quality engineers, technicians, operators, equipment specialists, facilities, safety, and logistics roles.
- Supply chain: materials, substrates, equipment service, calibration, consumables, logistics, customs, alternate sourcing, and FX exposure.
- Governance: applicable permits, environmental and social assessment, worker safety, quality systems, cybersecurity, IP, export controls, and data governance.

## 3. Phased work plan and gates

The durations are intentionally unspecified pending studies, procurement and regulatory guidance.

### Phase 0 — Scoping and baseline

**Activities**

- Verify applicant entity, governance, and decision authority.
- Request BIDA guidance on process and identify all relevant statutory authorities.
- Conduct market, customer, competitor, and product-scope research.
- Screen possible sites without representing any location as secured.
- Complete preliminary power, water, wastewater, logistics, environmental, and climate diligence.
- Build an initial risk register and a traceable assumptions/source register.

**Gate 0:** Commission a full feasibility study only if there is a credible market hypothesis, feasible site pathway, accountable sponsor, and funded study scope.

### Phase 1 — Feasibility and concept engineering

**Activities**

- Develop process flows and candidate package/service configurations.
- Obtain vendor budgetary quotations and equipment lead-time information.
- Prepare facility, cleanroom, utility, safety, environmental, and waste concepts based on selected processes.
- Develop customer qualification and quality-system requirements.
- Build an auditable CapEx, OpEx, working-capital, and cash-flow model with downside cases.
- Define workforce roles and training curricula; set headcount targets only after workload and shift models are prepared.
- Map permits, approvals, customs, tax, and incentive eligibility using current authoritative information and legal advice.

**Gate 1:** Continue only if independent review finds technical, market, site, workforce, regulatory, and financing assumptions credible enough for detailed engineering.

### Phase 2 — Pilot or demonstration capability

This phase may be considered only after project authorization, funding, site and safety approvals, customer requirements, equipment selection, staffing, and quality controls are documented.

**Activities**

- Procure and install approved equipment.
- Commission against vendor and engineering acceptance criteria.
- Train and qualify personnel.
- Run controlled engineering lots or demonstrations only under approved procedures and customer agreements.
- Record yield, cycle time, reliability, quality escapes, downtime, utilities consumption, and corrective actions.

**Gate 2:** No commercial production claim until customer qualification, regulatory requirements, operating permits, and independent readiness review are complete.

### Phase 3 — Commercial operation or expansion

Any scale-up requires evidence of qualified customer demand, accepted product quality, stable process capability, trained staffing, reliable utilities, environmental compliance, working capital, and a viable downside plan. Expansion into advanced packaging is a separate decision requiring its own process, customer, equipment, yield, thermal, reliability, IP, and financial evidence.

## 4. Capacity and workforce planning

No daily packaged-unit target, unit-per-hour estimate, annual output, or production ramp is established. A preliminary target such as **up to 0.5 million units per day** must not be used externally unless supported by product-specific line-balance calculations, equipment quotations, planned shifts, yield assumptions, downtime, qualification status, utilities, customer demand, and commissioning evidence.

Similarly, no headcount commitment is established. Plan staffing by work package and shift after the process model is known:

| Workforce group | Roles to scope | Target |
|---|---|---|
| VLSI/design engineers | Design enablement, test-program development, product engineering | To be derived from approved scope |
| Packaging/process engineers | Assembly, package/process integration, materials, reliability | To be derived from equipment and product mix |
| Quality engineers | Quality systems, metrology, failure analysis, customer qualification | To be derived from quality plan |
| Technicians and operators | Production, test, maintenance, cleanroom and traceability tasks | To be derived from shifts and automation |
| Equipment/facility specialists | Equipment service, calibration, utilities, EHS and facilities | To be derived from reliability and coverage model |

Training providers, vendor curricula, and university or industry partnerships are options to investigate, not existing commitments.

## 5. Commercial model and financing

Separate:

- **CapEx:** site, buildings, process/environmental infrastructure, production and test equipment, installation, commissioning, qualification, contingency, and financing during construction.
- **OpEx:** labor, utilities, materials, maintenance, logistics, calibration, compliance, insurance, training, and technical support.
- **Revenue:** service fees supported by customer evidence; distinguish inquiry, forecast, qualification, letter of interest, and binding order.
- **Public support:** identify only after current eligibility, conditions, timing, and written authority are verified.

Model NPV, IRR, payback, break-even utilization, debt-service capacity, working capital, FX exposure, and downside liquidity. Include sensitivities for construction delay, utilization, yield, price, order concentration, input costs, energy and water, FX, and financing. No project cost, government grant, tax holiday, VGF, duty exemption, or public funding is asserted by this draft.

## 6. Risk register

Ratings remain unrated until evidence and a documented scale are available.

| Risk | Early indicators | Candidate response |
|---|---|---|
| Permitting and schedule | Unclear approval sequence, unresolved critical-path dependencies | Confirm authority map early; maintain schedule contingency; do not bypass statutory review |
| Utility reliability | Unconfirmed capacity, quality, connection date, or water balance | Site-specific engineering and written utility diligence |
| Market and demand | No customer qualification pathway, concentrated or nonbinding demand | Independent customer validation; downside demand and utilization cases |
| Technology, yield, and quality | Unproven flow, equipment performance, reliability, or acceptance criteria | Vendor acceptance testing, controlled qualification, independent quality review |
| Workforce availability | Unvalidated skill supply, instructor capacity, or retention | Role-based workforce study, staged curricula, practical assessment |
| Imported supply and FX | Long lead times, single sources, currency mismatch | Map critical inputs, alternate suppliers, inventory and FX scenarios |
| Environmental and worker safety | Unknown water, waste, chemical, emissions, or occupational hazards | Baselines, permits, hazard analysis, controls, monitoring, and grievance process |
| Financing and policy | Reliance on unconfirmed incentives or financing | Exclude unconfirmed support from base case; stress test and document conditions |
| Cybersecurity and IP | Supplier exposure, data access, export-control concerns | Threat assessment, access limits, incident procedures, contractual IP controls |

## 7. Success measures

Establish baselines and targets only after feasibility, financing, authorizations, and an accountable owner exist. Candidate measures include:

- Study deliverables completed and independently reviewed.
- Permits and utility dependencies identified and resolved by accountable parties.
- Personnel trained and competency-assessed against defined roles.
- Equipment accepted and commissioned against documented criteria.
- Customer qualification milestones and contracted demand, reported distinctly.
- Yield, reliability, quality, delivery, utilization, and unit cost measured using declared definitions.
- Water, energy, emissions, waste, and worker-safety performance against approved baselines.

Illustrative milestones such as a cleanroom operating by Year 2, 200+ trained people, export operation by Year 5, or multiple customers are **not targets or commitments** unless supported by approved schedules, budgets, demand evidence, and authority. Do not publish them as promises.

## 8. Submission prerequisites

Before formal submission, attach only verified or explicitly qualified materials: applicant registration, signatory authority, current source register, market study, site and utilities screening, process concept, environmental and safety scoping, workforce baseline, financial model, risk register, and applicable approvals. Obtain Bangladesh legal, tax, engineering, and environmental review.

**This work plan is a feasibility framework. It does not establish BIDA approval, national-priority status, site control, eligibility for incentives, a funded pilot, production capacity, customers, or operating capability.**
